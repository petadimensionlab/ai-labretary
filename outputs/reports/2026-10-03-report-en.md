## 1. Purpose and Aim

### 1.1 What This Study Is Trying to Learn

In a kidney under sustained chronic inflammation, a structure known as a **tertiary lymphoid structure** (also called tertiary lymphoid tissue) can form: an aggregate of immune cells resembling a lymph node, in a place where no such organ belongs. PetadimensionLab's research question is how these structures bear on the course of kidney disease and kidney cancer, understood in connection with **cellular senescence**.

No wet-lab experiments are performed. **The work is done entirely in computation, using publicly available single-cell gene-expression data from human kidney together with Geneformer, a foundation model of gene expression.**

### 1.2 The Measurement Concept

Geneformer reads the measured gene expression of a single cell and maps that cell to a point in a 1,152-dimensional space (an *embedding*). If a gene is then artificially forced to high expression (**overexpression**), the point moves.

> If that gene genuinely matters for determining the cell's state, the point should move **toward the target cell state**.

This is called **in silico perturbation**: a way to ask "which genes, when activated, push a fibroblast toward the cell type that builds tertiary lymphoid structures?" without touching a cell.

### 1.3 Scope of This Report

**Between 2026-09-25 and 2026-10-03 this project designed 17 experiments and actually ran perturbation computations in 15 of them. Ten hypotheses received a verdict: 5 inconclusive, 4 refuted, and 1 partially supported.**

**That is, 5 of the 10 adjudicated hypotheses ended "inconclusive."**

"Inconclusive" means the hypothesis could be neither supported nor rejected. **In most cases the cause lay not in the biology but in how we were measuring.** Specifically:

- The **primary metric**, meant to capture whether a cell moved toward the target, was measuring the *magnitude* of movement rather than its *direction*
- The **positive control** that confirms the instrument is working failed to register
- Most importantly, **the decision criteria themselves were so strict that even a metric free of confounding would fail them 88% of the time**

**The last of these emerged only late, at the PI's prompting.** Until then the project was repeating a loop — fix the check that failed, re-run, watch a different check fail — without ever questioning the criteria themselves.

This report sets out **the judgment framework finally adopted and the first interpretable result obtained with it.** The trial and error leading up to that is placed in Appendix A.

## 2. The Research Question and Its Biological Background

### 2.1 Tertiary Lymphoid Structures in the Kidney

Lymph nodes and the spleen form at predetermined sites during fetal development. Tertiary lymphoid structures, by contrast, **form later, in tissue subjected to chronic inflammation.** In the kidney they have been reported after ischemia–reperfusion injury in aged kidneys, in chronic kidney disease, and around renal cell carcinoma.

When lymphoid tissue forms, **interstitial fibroblasts are known to convert into a state called "organizer" and to secrete factors that recruit immune cells.** In embryonic lymph node development, this conversion is driven centrally by the **non-canonical NF-κB pathway initiated by LTβR (lymphotoxin β receptor)**:

> **LTβR → NIK (gene symbol MAP3K14) → IKKα (gene symbol CHUK) → cleavage of p100 to p52 → nuclear entry with RelB**

Primary sources: Dejardin 2002 (PMID 12387745), Benezech 2012 (PMID 22940098), Chai-Onder 2013 (PMID 23623380), Sun 2017 (PMID 28580957).

**The question in this study is: if that pathway is pushed in kidney fibroblasts, which way does the cell state move?**

### 2.2 Data Used, and Its Constraints

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Data | KPMP Human Kidney Atlas v1.5, single-nucleus RNA sequencing |
| Source | Lake BB et al. 2023, Nature 619:585-594 (PMID 37468583) |
| Subset used | Fibroblast lineage, 21,769 cells |
| Model | Geneformer version 2 (V2-316M) |

**The heaviest constraint comes first. No histological label indicating the presence or absence of tertiary lymphoid structures exists in this dataset, nor in any other dataset surveyed.**

Consequently **it is not possible to measure directly whether a tertiary lymphoid structure formed.** What this study can measure is only the direction in which the cell state moved.

### 2.3 The Constraint Imposed by Single-Nucleus RNA Sequencing

Unlike single-cell RNA sequencing, which measures the whole cell, **single-nucleus RNA sequencing measures only RNA inside the nucleus.** Short transcripts that move rapidly into the cytoplasm and are secreted are poorly captured.

Measured directly, **the detection rate for chemokines characteristic of tertiary lymphoid organizers (CXCL13, CCL19, CCL21 and others) is only 1.1–9.3%**, while **extracellular matrix genes are detected at 33–52%.**

**In other words, with this assay the "inlet and plumbing" of the pathway are visible, but the "outlet" is not.** This is a property of the data itself, not something that better design can resolve.

Switching to other data was examined, but in the four single-cell datasets on hand, **fibroblasts were 5- to 13-fold fewer**, falling short of the required cell counts.

### 2.4 How the Target Cell State Was Defined

**This was the single most time-consuming design decision in the study.**

Initially we built our own score for "resemblance to a tertiary lymphoid organizer" and took the top-scoring cells as the target. **This is close to circular.** A target defined by our own score will naturally be approached by perturbing genes related to that score — and genes used to define the target can no longer serve as perturbation subjects.

The method finally adopted was to **use, unchanged, the annotation that the paper's authors assigned for a different purpose.**

| Role | Author's class | Cells | Donors |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Starting state** | Reference fibroblast (FIB) + medullary fibroblast (M-FIB) | **15,477** | 79 |
| **Target state** | Activated fibroblast (aFIB) + myofibroblast (MYOF) | **5,131** | 77 |

The authors (Lake et al. 2023, Supplementary Table 10) define this "activated stromal state" as **"cells differentiating into, or having become, high-extracellular-matrix-producing myofibroblasts and activated fibroblasts."**

**This choice carries both a gain and a loss.**

- **Gained**: no judgment of ours enters the genes, the scores, or the thresholds. Moreover, **the authors' supplementary tables list 42–76 genes characterizing the target state, allowing a positive control of adequate size for the first time**
- **Lost**: **the authors did not define this state as "the cells that build tertiary lymphoid structures."** That paper is not about tertiary lymphoid structures at all

Literature specific to kidney tertiary lymphoid structures was also examined, but **only about 12 independently verifiable marker genes could be found**, and these were chemokine-dominated and poorly captured by single-nucleus sequencing.

**The PI's decision (2026-09-30) was to "first establish the instrument on a state that can be measured."** But with a condition attached: **what this design can answer is whether fibroblasts become activated, not whether tertiary lymphoid structures form. That distinction must be stated explicitly in the report.**

## 3. Hypothesis

### 3.1 The Hypothesis Finally Tested

> **In kidney fibroblasts in the reference state (FIB + M-FIB), overexpressing each of 119 genes of the LTβR–non-canonical NF-κB pathway, one gene at a time, moves the cell embedding as a group toward the "activated stromal state" (aFIB + MYOF) as defined by the original authors.**

(Hypothesis ID: HYP-20261001-01. Gate G1 approved by the PI on 2026-10-01.)

### 3.2 How the Gene Set Was Constructed

A 120-gene set was used that had been **fixed in full, down to the ordering, from literature and databases alone, before a single line of this dataset was examined** (constructed in EXP-20260928-002). Its sources:

- GO:0038061 (non-canonical NF-κB signal transduction)
- Reactome "TNFR2 non-canonical NF-kB pathway"
- MSigDB `GOBP_NON_CANONICAL_NF_KAPPAB_SIGNAL_TRANSDUCTION`
- NF-κB target gene catalogues, plus the primary sources in §2.1

The composition is **61 pathway components** (MAP3K14, CHUK, TRAF3, NFKB1, upstream of RELB, and others), **13 transcriptional targets of the pathway** (CCL2, IL33, TIMP1 and others), and **45 genes from curated public database sets**.

Mechanical exclusion criteria were then applied, and **only one gene (FN1, overlapping the positive control) was removed, leaving 119.**

### 3.3 Why 119 Genes — Designing for Statistical Power

**This is the largest difference from all earlier attempts.**

The smallest effect detectable by perturbing a single gene is **2.73 standard deviations**, a level essentially never reached in practice. Judging **K genes as a set** lowers that floor.

| Set size K | Detection floor (effect detectable with 80% probability) |
|---|---|
| 1 | 2.73 |
| 11 | 2.00 |
| 18 | 1.56 |
| 39 | 1.03 |
| 71 | 0.78 |
| **119** | **0.606** |

**The previous experiment (EXP-20260930-001) tested 11 genes against a detection floor of 2.00, while observing 1.29–1.34.** Even a real effect of that size was **impossible to detect by design.**

**With 119 genes, the detection floor is 0.606 — comfortably below the magnitude observed previously.**

However, **this is not a prediction that the same effect size will appear.** The genes perturbed are different, so the effect size is unknown. What was pre-registered is only that **the design can detect an effect of 0.606 standard deviations or larger.**

### 3.4 What This Hypothesis Cannot Answer (**stated up front**)

- **Whether tertiary lymphoid structures form.** The target state is "activated stroma," not the cells that build tertiary lymphoid structures
- **What actually happens in humans or animals.** This is a computation on a foundation model's embeddings; results reach only the level of "suggested"
- **Anything about cellular senescence.** TP53, a central gene, remains under blinding and was not unblinded. It was not handled in this experiment, and there is no material on which to comment about senescence

## 4. The Judgment Framework — All Decision Criteria

### 4.1 Why Decision Criteria Are Necessary

What this study measures comes down to a single operation. The gene-expression profile of a kidney fibroblast is converted by a foundation model, Geneformer, into a point in a several-hundred-dimensional embedding space (the cell embedding). A gene is then artificially forced to a high expression rank (overexpression), and we measure how far that point moves toward the "target cell state." This is not a laboratory fact; it is a computational simulation called in silico perturbation.

For this measurement to mean anything, several preconditions must hold:

- **The axis must actually exist.** The direction labeled "toward the target state" must be a meaningful direction in the model's embedding space, and this must be confirmed in advance by perturbing known driver genes (positive controls) and checking that they move along that direction.
- **Confounding must be absent, or at least measured and subtracted.** Factors with no biological meaning—how far a gene's expression rank had to be pushed, how many genes were detected in a given cell (input length)—must not be allowed to masquerade as the effect of interest.
- **Circularity must be avoided.** Genes used to define the target state must not be reused as "evidence that the effect occurred."
- **Statistical power (the detection floor) must be sufficient.** Even a real and meaningful effect cannot be detected if the gene set is too small; this is a design-time limitation known in advance, not a measurement error.

These preconditions break down in three main ways: (1) confounding, where a quantity treated as a control turns out to correlate with the outcome, so that bias is mistaken for effect; (2) circularity, where genes used to define the target are also used to measure approach to the target, producing a tautology; and (3) underpowering, where a set is too small to say anything except "inconclusive" or "no effect at this detection floor," regardless of whether a true effect exists.

This project has designed 17 experiments, of which 15 included running the perturbation computation (the remaining two were a pre-feasibility check and a re-evaluation, with no perturbation at all). Of these, 10 hypotheses reached an adjudicated verdict: **5 inconclusive, 4 refuted**, and 1 partially supported. That is, 5 of the 10 adjudicated verdicts ended "inconclusive." Tracing the cause back, in most cases the fault lay not in the measured quantity itself but in **the soundness-check criteria used to validate the measurement.** When the PI directed a retrospective audit of the old checks (M1–M5, discussed below) on 2026-10-02, it turned out that even on a "clean metric"—one that by construction contains no confounding—the full battery of checks passed only 12% of the time. In other words, a perfectly sound metric would fail this battery 88% of the time, and the project had been using exactly this battery across all six inconclusive verdicts **standing as of the audit date, 2026-10-02** (one of which was later resolved to "refuted" after the control pool was enlarged).

The principle this audit finally arrived at is this:

> **Criteria are derived from the judgment they are meant to protect. The value of the primary metric is never used to justify the criteria.**

Before this, neither the choice of checks nor their numeric thresholds had any traceable derivation beyond "this is what the previous experiment used." For some thresholds, no derivation was ever recorded. The rebuilt checks (S-1 through S-4, below) all derive their numbers from a single question fixed in advance: "how much could this set's mean value move from confounding alone, and how much of the judgment threshold we are trying to protect (the set's absolute-value condition of 0.41 standard deviations) would that erosion consume?"

### 4.2 Definition of the Primary Metric

#### 4.2.1 Final Form

The primary metric is defined as follows:

> Take the displacement vector of the cell embedding before and after perturbation (post-perturbation point minus pre-perturbation point). Project it onto the unit vector pointing from the centroid of the starting state to the centroid of the target state. Divide the length of that projection by the magnitude of the displacement vector itself (the amount of movement).

This is equivalent to the cosine of the angle between the displacement and the direction toward the target. A larger value means a larger fraction of the perturbation-induced movement points toward the target.

#### 4.2.2 Why Not the Difference Between Two Centroid-Approach Scores

An earlier design used the difference between "approach to the target centroid" and "approach to the starting-state centroid" as the primary metric. Empirically, this form had two problems.

**First, the sign changes depending on which state is subtracted.** Perturbations carry a uniform "pulling strength" toward any centroid, regardless of which gene is perturbed, and this strength differs by state (observed: +0.09–0.12 toward the target centroid, +0.18–0.22 toward the starting-state centroid, +0.52 toward the centroid of degenerated medullary fibroblasts). Simply changing what is subtracted—from the average of three states to the starting state alone—flipped the rank correlation between the residual and the movement magnitude from +0.009–+0.049 to −0.122 to −0.148. The subtraction operation itself reverses the sign of the result depending on an arbitrary choice of reference.

**Second, it correlates arithmetically with the neutrality metric.** The difference-form primary metric and the uniform component (a diagnostic quantity meant to capture perturbations that approach every centroid equally) are built from the same two underlying quantities: approach to the target and approach to the starting state. The difference (target − start) and the sum/average (the uniform component) are linear combinations of the same two variables, so they are mathematically guaranteed to correlate. The observed correlation between the two underlying quantities (0.937–0.944) and their variance ratio (1.23–1.25) predict a theoretical correlation of 0.28–0.30, which matched the observed correlation to four decimal places. **This is not confounding, so it cannot be removed by confound correction.** Building a metric that structurally correlates with its own soundness check—in a way no correction can remove—undermines the soundness check itself.

Because of these two defects, the primary metric was changed to the projection form, and the difference form was dropped.

#### 4.2.3 Removing Confounding

Two factors with no biological meaning can leak into the primary metric:

- **Original rank** (where the gene sat in the cell's input ranking before the overexpression operation). Because overexpression moves a gene to the top rank, genes that started lower have to move further—an artifact of the mechanics of the operation, not of biology.
- **Input length** (roughly, the number of genes detected and tokenized for that cell). Cells with more detected genes can behave differently under the same operation.

To remove these, the primary metric uses the **residual from a regression fit on control genes** (a large set of genes assumed to have no effect). The regression's predictors are a full quadratic in original rank and input length. For genes under evaluation, the same fitted equation (with coefficients estimated from controls only) is used to compute an expected value, which is subtracted to obtain the residual. One rule is essential here: **a gene being evaluated is always excluded from the control pool used to fit its own regression.** Including the gene in its own reference set could artificially neutralize it through self-reference.

#### 4.2.4 Why Movement-Magnitude Correction Is Not Applied by Default

Movement magnitude (‖Δ‖, how far the embedding actually moved) is itself a candidate confounder. Adding it as a regression predictor ("movement correction") is **not applied by default**, because its effect depends on the rest of the design.

In an earlier design (EXP-20260929-001, using the difference-form metric and subtracting the average of three states), adding movement correction pulled the set-average value of housekeeping genes (expected to be neutral, with no reason to approach the target) from 1.071 down to 0.335—exactly the intended correction. Taken alone, this looks like strong evidence that the correction works.

However, in a later design (EXP-20260930-001, using the projection-form metric), **housekeeping genes were already within the neutral range without any movement correction**, and adding it made the soundness checks worse instead (the rank correlation with the uniform component rose from 0.31 to 0.40, and the rank correlation with movement magnitude worsened from −0.07 to −0.12). In other words, an improvement that worked under one design did not generalize to another. **Whenever the design changes—the form of the primary metric, or which states are subtracted—whether a given correction is still needed must be re-measured, not assumed.** Because of this lesson, movement correction is off by default, and any decision to include it must be accompanied by a stated reason and measured evidence.

#### 4.2.5 Why the Uniform Component Is Built from States Not Used in the Primary Metric

The uniform component (a diagnostic quantity capturing perturbations that approach every state's centroid equally) is built as the average approach toward centroids of states that are *not* used in the primary metric's projection (here, degenerated fibroblasts and degenerated medullary fibroblasts), rather than from the starting and target states. This avoids the problem described in §4.2.2: building the primary metric and the uniform component from the same two underlying quantities guarantees an arithmetic correlation. If the two shared the same ingredients, their correlation would be a mathematical artifact, not a meaningful soundness signal.

#### 4.2.6 Why Cells with Zero Movement Are Excluded

Overexpression perturbation raises a gene's expression rank to the top of the input. If a gene already occupied the top rank before perturbation, the operation changes the input by not a single bit, and the pre- and post-perturbation embeddings are identical. The displacement vector's magnitude (movement) is then exactly zero.

Empirically, 114 of 338,967 cells (0.034%) fell into this category, and **in every one of them, the perturbed gene's original rank was already at the top (rank 1).** This is an inevitable consequence of the perturbation mechanism, not a flaw in the metric. But mixing zero-movement cells into the primary metric either leaves the cosine undefined (division by zero) or, if treated as zero, dilutes the measured effect uniformly. Zero-movement cells are therefore excluded as cases where "no perturbation occurred." It is essential that the same set of excluded cells be used across every version being compared (with or without a given correction, etc.); otherwise, a difference in exclusion criteria would masquerade as a difference between versions.

### 4.3 Soundness Checks S-1 through S-4 (The Final Set)

The four soundness checks (S-1 through S-4) all answer the question "is it valid to interpret the primary metric's value (the set mean)?" **All four must pass before the primary metric's value is used as grounds for any reported conclusion.**

#### S-1: Bias Budget

**Definition.** Five candidate confounders are enumerated: movement magnitude (median ‖Δ‖ per gene), original rank (log2(1+rank)), input length (log2(input length)), relative position (rank ÷ input length), and the uniform component (per-gene residual). Using control genes only, the standardized residual z is regressed **jointly** on the quantiles (rank-transformed to 0–1) of all five candidates.

For the set under evaluation (e.g., the 119-gene intervention set), the "bias" contributed by each candidate v is

> bias b_v = (joint-regression partial coefficient fitted on controls) × (the set's mean quantile for v − 0.5)

and the total bias is B = Σ b_v. The set passes if **the upper bound of the 95% confidence interval for |B| (i.e., |B| + 1.96 × standard error)** is at most the budget of 0.10 standard deviations, in all three seeds.

**Why joint regression rather than marginal regression (fitting each candidate separately).** The five candidates are strongly intercorrelated (rank correlations among controls, observed range 0.44–0.88; e.g., movement vs. original rank 0.873–0.878, movement vs. relative position 0.780–0.787, movement vs. uniform component 0.436–0.459, uniform component vs. original rank 0.237–0.264). Summing marginally fitted b_v values counts the same underlying bias multiple times. On synthetic data, **the marginal-regression Σ|b_v| overstated the true bias by a factor of 3.6–4.3.** Worse, on a "clean metric" containing no confounding at all (projection of the 1,152-dimensional embedding onto a random direction), a set whose quantiles were skewed to 0.75 had a **0% pass rate under the marginal-regression version** (versus 51.7% under the joint-regression version). Using marginal regression would repeat the same failure mode seen before: a sound metric gets rejected. Judgment therefore uses joint regression (partial coefficients); the marginal-regression Σ|b_v| is reported only as a "double-counted upper bound" and is never used to decide pass/fail.

**Derivation of the 0.10 threshold**, in five steps, not inherited from past practice:

1. The judgment to be protected is the set's absolute-value condition, **0.41 standard deviations**, fixed by the PI before the results were seen.
2. Bias is required to stay within one-quarter of this judgment: 0.41 ÷ 4 = 0.1025 → rounded to **0.10**.
3. As a fraction of the judgment threshold: 0.10 / 0.41 = **24.4%**.
4. Compared against irreducible noise—the standard deviation of the set-mean z obtained by repeatedly drawing random 119-gene sets from controls alone (observed 0.0802 / 0.0782 / 0.0806 across three seeds)—0.10 / 0.080 ≈ **1.25×**. This keeps systematic bias at roughly the same scale as the chance variability the design already tolerates.
5. Compared against the standard error of the statistic B itself—the median se_B from 1,500 random 119-gene control sets, **0.0073**—0.10 / 0.0073 ≈ **13.7×**. That is, the 0.10 budget is comfortably larger than measurement precision, and so is actually **measurable**.

**Checking the upper end.** Loosening the budget to half the judgment threshold (0.205) would let bias alone move half the judgment. Tightening it to one-eighth (0.051) falls below the 95th percentile of random control-set noise (observed 0.0742), meaning even unbiased sets would start failing. The adopted value, 0.10, lies between these two extremes.

**Why this matters.** Points 4 and 5 holding simultaneously is the crux: the budget must be ≤0.10 to protect the judgment (points 2–4), and 0.10 is 13.7× measurement precision, so it is actually measurable (point 5). The old check, M5 (discussed below), satisfied the analogue of point 5 by only 2.1×—it demanded a precision finer than the measurement could deliver.

**Limitation.** This method only estimates bias explainable by the five enumerated candidates. It cannot establish "no confounding," only "bias from the enumerated candidates is below 0.10 standard deviations." When candidates are noisy proxies for a true, unobserved confounder, recovery of the true bias in simulation ranged from 87% to 95%.

#### S-2: Binomial Test on Sham Sets

**Definition.** Two hundred "sham sets" are constructed from control genes, matched to the evaluated set on properties such as expression frequency, position in the input, input length, and cell count. Each is passed through the same pass/fail rule. Using the empirically measured chance pass rate p for a single gene drawn from controls, a binomial test (k ~ Binomial(200, p)) is applied to the number of sham sets that pass, k, and the set is judged acceptable if the one-sided p-value is ≥ 0.05 (i.e., consistent with chance).

**Why "zero of N sets passing" is not used as the requirement.** An earlier phase of the project required "zero of 200 sham sets pass." But when the expected number of chance passes was 1.12, demanding exactly zero meant **a well-designed, unbiased setup would fail this requirement 67% of the time.** A few chance passes are expected and should be evaluated via a binomial test for consistency with chance, not treated as automatic failure.

**Limitation.** For the K=119 intervention set, the chance pass rate is on the order of 5×10⁻⁶, giving an expected pass count among 200 sham sets of only 0.001. At this scale the test has almost no power. Passing this check does not mean "the sham sets failed, therefore the real result is genuine"; this limitation is stated explicitly in the report.

#### S-3: Positive Control on the Goal Axis

**Definition.** The set-average value for 71 marker genes characterizing the target state (the activated interstitial state) must exceed the 99th percentile of the control-set distribution, and its absolute value must be at least 0.53 standard deviations, in all three seeds.

**Why this is necessary.** This check asks whether the "direction toward the target" even exists as a meaningful axis in this embedding space. Without passing it, there is no basis for interpreting the primary metric at all. In this project, this gauge **failed to register in four consecutive experiments** — EXP-20260927-004, EXP-20260928-001, EXP-20260928-002, and EXP-20260929-001, with positive-control gene counts of 1, 24, 0, and 7 respectively — before first passing in EXP-20260930-001 (set average 1.002 / 1.093 / 1.067).

**Why the upper bound of bias is subtracted before judging.** The 71 positive-control genes are strongly skewed in their properties (mean quantile among controls: uniform component 0.217, input length 0.334, movement 0.457, original rank 0.545, relative position 0.668). Applying the S-1 bias budget (0.10) directly to such a skewed set would always fail (observed B = +0.126 / +0.129 / +0.150, upper bound 0.238 / 0.239 / 0.258)—but that reflects the fact that the positive control set is inherently skewed, which is a different matter from whether the gauge itself is working. The check therefore uses the positive-control value minus the upper bound of its own bias estimate. Subtracting the upper bound from the previously observed values (0.976 / 1.057 / 1.029) gives 0.718 / 0.818 / 0.771, comfortably above the required 0.53. **Even under the most conservative estimate of bias, the gauge is registering.**

**Derivation of the detection floor, 0.78.** Obtained by simulating the pre-registered decision rule itself: for a set of size K=71, the effect size detectable with 80% probability, incorporating observed between-gene variability (τ = 2.051, see §4.5), is 0.782 standard deviations.

#### S-4: Set-Level Neutrality

**Definition.** The set-average value for the four adopted neutral controls must fall within the 0.05–0.95 quantile range of the random control-set distribution in at least two of the three seeds.

**Adoption rules H-1 through H-3 for neutral controls (fixed numerically before seeing results).**

| Rule | Condition | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| H-1 | Detection rate in both starting and target states | ≥ 0.20 |
| H-2 | Absolute value of the expression bias (log-ratio of detection rates) between states | ≤ 0.25 |
| H-3 | Coefficient of variation of detection rate across donors | ≤ 0.50 |

**Why rules are necessary.** Housekeeping genes (e.g., ACTB, GAPDH, B2M) have traditionally been used as neutral controls under the assumption that they are expressed at a constant level in every cell and therefore have no reason to approach the target state. **This assumption does not hold in single-nucleus RNA sequencing (snRNA-seq).** Observed detection rates for B2M were 0.469 (starting state) and 0.425 (target state); for GAPDH, 0.197 and 0.133. Because snRNA-seq captures nuclear, not cytoplasmic, transcripts, even canonically "constant" genes can be detected unreliably.

**Result of applying the rules.** Applying H-1 through H-3 mechanically to 11 candidates (4 housekeeping genes and 7 neutral-control-panel genes) yielded **only 4 adopted genes: ACTB, CERS4, ANAPC16, and EEF1D.** GAPDH failed all three conditions; B2M failed because its coefficient of variation, 0.5002, exceeded the 0.50 threshold.

**Disclosure of the near-miss.** B2M's coefficient of variation (0.5002) and that of VAPA, a neutral-control-panel candidate (0.5015), both exceed the 0.50 threshold only marginally. **The threshold was not adjusted in response to this near-miss**—doing so would defeat the purpose of fixing the rule in advance. Instead, a version with the threshold relaxed to 0.55 (adding B2M and VAPA, for 6 genes total) was pre-registered as a sensitivity analysis **before results were seen.**

### 4.4 Dropped Checks and Why They Were Dropped

On 2026-10-02, at the PI's direction, the old checks M1 through M5 were audited. The audit used 60 "clean metrics"—metrics guaranteed by construction to be free of confounding and circularity, obtained simply by projecting the displacement vector onto 60 random directions in the 1,152-dimensional embedding space, unrelated to the actual confound-removal pipeline. Applying the old checks M1–M5 to these 60 clean metrics gave the following pass counts:

| Check | Pass rate (of 60) | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| M1 (calibration of the control-gene rank distribution) | 60 / 60 | Does not trigger regardless of injected defects. Carries no information |
| M2 (rank correlation of residual with original position and input length) | — | Undetectable in principle. Since the confound-removal regression's predictors are exactly the full quadratic in original rank and input length, correlations with these quantities are absorbed by definition. Even artificially injecting a ±0.30 defect left the statistic unchanged to four decimal places |
| M3 (rank of the four individual neutral controls) | 43 / 60 | Four genes is too few; the signal is swamped by noise. No derivation for the threshold was ever recorded |
| M4 (rank correlation of residual with the uniform component) | 49 / 60 | No derivation for the threshold was ever recorded |
| **M5 (rank correlation of residual with movement magnitude)** | **8 / 60 (13%)** | The threshold of 0.10 is only **2.1×** the statistic's own standard error (0.045–0.047)—a level of precision the measurement cannot deliver |
| **All five passing together** | **7 / 60 (12%)** | Even a metric completely free of confounding **fails at least one check 88% of the time** |

**The significance of this 88% figure is substantial.** If the probability of failing on any single run is 88%, the probability of failing on six runs is 0.88⁶ ≈ **46%**. In other words, the six "inconclusive" results **standing as of 2026-10-02** fell well within the range expected even if the underlying metric was perfectly sound. This does not mean the metric was flawed and caused the repeated inconclusive outcomes; it means **the check battery itself was constructed in a way that would reject a sound metric.**

**Why each check failed to function:**

- **M2 (original rank, input length) is undetectable in principle**, because the confound-removal regression's formula is exactly that quadratic. The correction absorbs any such defect by construction, so passing this check demonstrates nothing about the absence of confounding.
- **M1 does not trigger at this project's scale** (roughly 500 control genes). No injected defect caused its value to cross the threshold.
- **M3 is swamped by noise with only four genes (neutral controls)**: even a sound metric produces highly variable individual-gene ranks, and four genes provide insufficient stability for a judgment.
- **M5 and the "original rank" component of M2 were measuring substantially the same thing.** Their correlation was **0.978**, driven by the fact that, among control genes, movement magnitude and original rank are themselves strongly correlated (0.873–0.878). Two checks that appeared independent were in fact carrying one piece of information.
- **The thresholds were not calibrated against each other.** Comparing, for a fixed biased set, the bias each check's threshold would tolerate as a fraction of the judgment line (0.41 standard deviations): M5 tolerated 36%, M4 tolerated 104%, and M2 (relative position) tolerated 151%. M4 and M2 (relative position) let through bias large enough to overturn the judgment, while M5 alone was excessively strict.
- **M5's threshold of 0.10 was only 2.1× the statistic's own standard error (0.045–0.047).** The instability this produces is visible directly: between two experiments whose embeddings did not differ by a single bit (i.e., computed from the identical model run), simply swapping 59% of the control-gene roster shifted M5's value from −0.078 to −0.109, **flipping the pass/fail decision.** A check that reverses its verdict purely because of how controls happen to be drawn—with the measured object itself unchanged—cannot be relied on.

These checks are no longer used to decide pass/fail, but their values are retained and reported descriptively in full. "Dropping" a check means removing it from the pass/fail decision, not deleting it from the record.

### 4.5 Designing for Statistical Power

**The detection floor is the smallest true effect size (in units of control-gene standard deviation) that a given decision rule can detect with 80% probability.** It is obtained by directly simulating the pre-registered decision rule itself—random draws from controls, the 99th-percentile condition, the absolute-value condition, and the requirement that all three seeds pass—rather than by any closed-form approximation.

**Why a crude formula (4.5/√K, where K is the set size) is not used in reporting.** Such a formula is useful as a rough planning-stage guide, but it diverges systematically from direct simulation. For a set of size K=11 (the predecessor to the current intervention set), the formula gives 1.36 standard deviations, while direct simulation of the actual decision rule gave **2.00 standard deviations**. For a K=39 set (a subset of positive controls from a separate experiment), the formula gives 0.69, while simulation gave **1.03**. The formula underestimates systematically because it omits two quantities observed in the data: between-gene variability in effect size (observed τ = 2.051) and the correlation between seeds (observed around 0.944). Because of this, the detection floor is always computed by simulating the actual decision rule; the simple formula is confined to early planning and never used in reporting.

**Observed relationship between set size K and detection floor:**

| K | Detection floor (SD) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | 2.00 | `EXP-20260930-001/work/power_004.json` (set T1) |
| 18 | 1.56 | same source (set T1_T2) |
| 39 | 1.03 | same source (positive_control39); note the same-named set in `EXP-20261001-001/work/power_005.json`, computed against a different control pool, gives 1.06 |
| 71 | 0.78 | `EXP-20261001-001/work/power_005.json` (positive_control71) |
| 119 | 0.604 | same source (INT, the primary judgment) |

Larger K produces a smaller detection floor, making the same true effect easier to detect. The primary judgment's detection floor of 0.604 (K=119) is about **3.3 times smaller** than the detection floor for the K=11 set used in earlier experiments (2.00)—this difference in statistical power is the single largest design change separating this experiment from its predecessors.

**A single gene cannot be reliably assessed.** The detection floor for a single gene (effectively K=1) reaches 2.73 standard deviations, far exceeding even the largest effects observed in this project (roughly 1.0 standard deviation for the positive-control set). This is why the unit of judgment was placed at the level of the gene set rather than the individual gene.

**The pre-feasibility check (`scripts/analysis/axis_feasibility.py`).** Before any perturbation is run, this check determines whether the target axis can be measured at all under the proposed design. The single strongest condition is **a positive-control gene count K ≥ 20**; designs falling below this are not attempted. Empirically, a K=7 positive-control set has a detection floor of 1.71 standard deviations, and **no positive-control set in this project has ever reached that level.** No amount of careful check design can rescue a set too small to detect the effect it is meant to detect. The check returns one of three verdicts—pass, hold-for-review, or fail. A fail blocks the start of the experiment until the design is reworked; a hold-for-review requires the identified weaknesses to be documented in the plan and referred to the PI for judgment.

### 4.6 Discipline of Pre-Registration

Pre-registration means fixing, in a document (`plan.md`, `config.yaml`), the decision criteria, the target genes, and the cell-selection method *before* results are seen. The fixed content is recorded as a sha256 hash (`work/SHA256SUMS-preregistered.txt`) and verified from within the experiment directory. This verification mechanically confirms that not a single byte has changed; it does not rely on memory or self-report.

**Any change made after fixing the pre-registration requires all three of the following:**

1. Record the date, content, reason, and **whether the change was made before or after seeing results** in a change log.
2. Update the pre-registration checklist.
3. Re-compute the sha256 hashes and re-fix until every file matches again.

**Criteria are never changed after seeing results.** Even when a check turns out to be inappropriate, the response is not to draw a conclusion from the flawed check's output, but to redesign the check, **re-fix the redesigned check before seeing results again**, and only then re-run the evaluation. When S-1 through S-4 were rebuilt in this project, the existing perturbation data (embeddings already obtained in EXP-20261001-001) were not re-generated at all; only the definitions of the checks were re-fixed in advance, and the data were then re-evaluated (EXP-20261002-001).

**Re-evaluations performed after seeing results are always disclosed as such.** The report states plainly, "this is not pre-registered analysis performed before running the perturbation, but a re-evaluation of existing data." Throughout this project's reporting, every number is marked as having been fixed before results were seen, or as arising from a post-hoc re-evaluation.

## 6. What This Result Does and Does Not Establish

### 6.1 What It Establishes

> **In kidney fibroblasts in the reference state, overexpressing each of 119 genes of the LTβR–non-canonical NF-κB pathway did not produce a group-level movement of the cell embedding toward the "activated stromal state" of 0.606 standard deviations or larger.**

### 6.2 What It Does Not Establish

- **It does not establish that there is no effect.** Effects below the detection floor of 0.606 standard deviations are not excluded. The observed values were 0.146–0.261, and **an effect of that magnitude is neither confirmed nor ruled out**
- **It does not establish that tertiary lymphoid structures do not form.** What was measured is movement toward the activated stromal state, not the formation of a lymphoid structure
- **It does not establish that the pathway is inactive in kidney.** Single-nucleus sequencing barely sees the pathway's outlet (secreted chemokines). **What was measured is the inlet and the plumbing**
- **It says nothing about cellular senescence.** Blinding was not lifted and the relevant genes were not handled

### 6.3 Two Points That Must Be Stated Plainly

**1. The margin on seed 82 is 0.003.** Against a bias budget of 0.10, the 95% upper bound was 0.0970. **The probability of passing had been estimated in advance at about 0.72; this passed by a narrow margin.**

**2. The positive control is not an independent replication.** Because the replicate seeds were kept identical to the preceding experiment, the positive control and neutral controls are computed from **exactly the same cells and the same embeddings** as before. Only the form of the metric and the control pool changed. **It cannot be written that "the positive control registered twice in succession."**

---

## 7. What Should Be Measured Next

**This project has only now obtained a working instrument.** Three directions follow, in order.

1. **Lower the detection floor.** It currently stands at 0.606. Bringing it to a level able to adjudicate the observed 0.146–0.261 (roughly 0.15) would require a far larger set, more replicates, or more cells per gene. **However, reaching below 0.15 appears to require K above 900, which is not practical. A different design is needed**
2. **Find data in which the pathway's outlet is measurable.** Single-nucleus sequencing cannot see the chemokines. Spatial transcriptomics (for example the Visium data of Meylan 2022) has not been examined
3. **Redefine a target closer to tertiary lymphoid structures, still established independently.** The current target is "activated stroma," and a gap to the original question remains

**Cellular senescence requires a fresh design, including a decision on lifting the blinding.**

### A.2　The walls we hit repeatedly

Grouped by what the obstacle was, not by date. There were at least six.

### Wall 1　Deletion moves every gene in the same direction

In the first experiment (EXP-20260926-001), whatever gene we deleted, the cell moved away from all three state centroids by a comparable amount — in every one of 90 control genes × seeds. About 56% of this is a geometric second-order term: any displacement of a vector takes it further from every nearby point.

The uniform movement can be subtracted. Doing so brought the fraction of control genes moving away from the goal down from 1.00 to 0.42–0.50. But subtraction alone did not establish the positive controls. What finally removed the uniform movement was fine-tuning: in the fine-tuned model the fraction fell to 0.10–0.15. **That only eliminated the uniform movement, however; the separate problem that the positive controls would not stand up remained** (HAVCR1 failed in EXP-20260926-003).

### Wall 2　The metric measured distance, not direction

Overexpression carries a component that moves a cell closer to *every* state centroid. In EXP-20260927-003, measured across control genes, the correlation between approach-to-goal and approach-to-an-unrelated-state was 0.946 / 0.913 / 0.915 across three seeds. **If approach to a single centroid is the primary metric, what is being measured is not "did it head for the goal" but "how far did it move."**

The fix was to subtract the mean approach to the other three centroids and divide by the displacement magnitude (EXP-20260927-004). This dropped the correlation with the uniform component from 0.979 to 0.106. It also had a side effect: the share of control-gene scatter that is gene-specific and reproduces across seeds rose from 32% to 82%, so **a rank-only decision rule no longer reduced its chance pass rate by adding seeds** (probability of at least one pass among 10 candidates: 0.665). We added an absolute size condition — at least 2.5 control standard deviations — to the rule.

### Wall 3　A single gene does not provide enough power

Pre-analysis for EXP-20260928-001 showed that, to first order, the primary metric equals the inner product of a gene's mean displacement direction with a fixed axis determined by the centroids (direction alone explains 81–85% of the variance). That displacement direction is near-fixed across seeds (cosine 0.985, minimum 0.908).

In other words, **the spread of the control genes is not statistical noise but a scatter of directions on a sphere, so it does not shrink with more cells or more seeds.** Empirically, doubling the cells per gene from 100 to 200 shrank the control standard deviation only 5%, from 3.19 × 10⁻⁵ to 3.04 × 10⁻⁵.

**The single-gene detection floor is 2.73 SD, and there is no way to lower it.** The only remaining move was to raise the unit of judgement. Judging a set of genes brings the floor to 0.54 for 10 genes and 0.32 for 24. The 10 positive-control candidates of EXP-20260927-004 already carried an effect of 1.17 / 1.17 / 1.00 as a set. **They had simply been undetectable one gene at a time.**

### Wall 4　Housekeeping genes refuse to be neutral

Housekeeping genes (ACTB, GAPDH, PPIA) serve as negative controls on the premise that, since they operate uniformly in every cell, perturbing them should not move the cell state. **That premise broke in four experiments** (EXP-20260926-001 and -005, EXP-20260927-003, EXP-20260929-001). Each time, the pre-registered rule forced an inconclusive verdict.

We explained the cause wrongly once. On 2026-09-27 we wrote that it was "because there is more room to push them up," but the three genes sit at nearly the same original relative position (ACTB 0.989, GAPDH 0.981–0.984, PPIA 0.972–0.974), so that explanation does not hold. **The real cause was the magnitude of the perturbation-induced displacement.** The three genes' displacements sit at the 97.8th–99.8th percentile of the control distribution, and the metric retained a correlation of +0.171 with displacement.

As a remedy we added log displacement to the de-confounding regression. On the EXP-20260929-001 data it worked: the three-gene set mean went from 1.071 to 0.335, and the number of seeds inside the neutral range went from 0/3 to 2/3. **But the correction became unnecessary once the design changed — and then made things worse.** In EXP-20260930-001, with no correction at all, ACTB sat at 0.834 / 0.840 / 0.863 and GAPDH at 0.753 / 0.740 / 0.792, both inside the range. The real reason they returned to neutrality was the rebuilt goal definition and subtraction scheme, not the correction. Leaving the correction in degraded two other diagnostics. We removed it in the end.

B2M, by contrast, stayed off-scale (0.0027–0.0054) no matter how the metric was rebuilt. As the light chain of MHC class I it rises sharply with inflammation, so it may simply have been unsuitable as a housekeeping control. An adoption rule fixed before looking at the results relegated it to descriptive reporting.

### Wall 5　The goal-axis positive control would not stand up

This happened four times running. **A positive control that will not stand up means there is no gene known to move cells along the goal direction — that is, the ruler has no scale on it.** Without a scale, a small primary value cannot be told apart from an unmeasurable one.

For these four runs, Labretary initially explained that "the goal is too close to measure." **That explanation was wrong.** The ratio of start-to-goal distance over start-to-myofibroblast distance lies between 0.442 and 0.637 across the four experiments. EXP-20260928-001, which earned a qualified pass, sits at 0.452; EXP-20260929-001, which failed, sits at 0.442 — **essentially the same. This quantity did not separate success from failure.**

The real cause was **the number of genes used as positive controls**: 1 (EXP-20260927-004), 0 (EXP-20260928-002), 7 (EXP-20260929-001) and 24 (EXP-20260928-001). As Wall 3 explains, one gene gives a detection floor of 2.73 SD and seven genes still 1.71 SD. **No positive control in this project ever reached 1.71 SD.** And at the time of EXP-20260929-001 there were 227 eligible candidates. Seven was a choice, not a constraint.

In response we built a pre-commitment feasibility check (`scripts/analysis/axis_feasibility.py`) that decides, **without running a single perturbation**, whether the design can measure the intended axis, and fails any design with fewer than 20 positive-control genes. Applied retrospectively to the four earlier experiments it returns three failures, one hold, and zero passes. **This project had never once had a design in which the goal axis was unconditionally measurable.**

The breakthrough came with EXP-20260930-001. We stopped defining the goal with a home-made score, defined it instead from the original authors' cell annotations (Lake et al. 2023, PMID 37468583), and took 71 positive controls from the same paper's supplementary tables. Their median detection rate in the goal cells in single-nucleus data was 0.321 — four to ten times the 0.039–0.088 of the previous four experiments. The result: **the 71-gene positive control set reached 1.002 / 1.093 / 1.067 SD, and for the first time the goal axis had a scale.** All six sensitivity analyses agreed.

### Wall 6　The acceptance criteria themselves were not valid

This was the last and largest problem to surface.

Once six hypotheses had been declared inconclusive because one of the pre-registered soundness checks had failed — **the count as it stood on 2026-10-02** — the PI directed us to audit the checks themselves. (One of those six, HYP-20261001-01, was later settled as refuted by EXP-20261002-002; every figure in this section is as of the audit on 2026-10-02.) We built 60 "clean" metrics — displacement vectors projected onto random directions in the 1,152-dimensional embedding space, which cannot contain confounding by construction — and ran them through all five checks.

| Check (internal name) | What it measures | Passes out of 60 |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | Calibration of the control rank distribution | 60 / 60 (never fires on any defect; carries no information) |
| M2 (original rank, input length) | Rank correlation of the residual with these | Undetectable in principle: the de-confounding regression is a full quadratic in them, so they are absorbed by definition. Injecting a ±0.30 defect does not change the statistic to four decimal places |
| M2 (relative position) | Rank correlation with relative position | 58 / 60 |
| M3 | Ranks of the four individual neutral controls | 43 / 60 |
| M4 | Rank correlation with the uniform component | 49 / 60 |
| M5 | Rank correlation with displacement magnitude | **8 / 60 (13%)** |
| All five together | — | **7 / 60 (12%)** |

**We had been using a test battery that rejects 88% of confounding-free metrics.** At a per-run failure probability of 0.88, six failures occur with probability 0.88⁶ = 46%. **The six inconclusive verdicts standing on 2026-10-02 were entirely consistent with a perfectly sound metric.**

The thresholds were also out of balance. For the same exposure (a 0.9 quantile within the controls), the distortion imposed on the set mean, expressed against the decision line of 0.41 SD, was:

| Check | Threshold | Distortion at threshold ÷ 0.41 |
|---|---|---|
| M5 (displacement) | 0.10 | 36% |
| M4 (uniform component) | 0.40 | 104% |
| M2 (relative position) | 0.25 | 151% |

**M5 alone was excessively strict, while M4 and M2 were letting through distortions large enough to overturn the conclusion** — and neither M4's nor M5's threshold had any recorded derivation.

They also overlapped. M5 and M2 (original rank) correlate at 0.978: the same thing measured in different coordinates. The reason is that perturbation displacement is nearly the same quantity as "how low the gene originally sat" (rank correlation 0.873–0.878 across 500 controls). M2 is absorbed exactly by the regression and therefore always passes, while M5, which measures the same content, always fails.

Finally, M5's threshold of 0.10 is only **2.1 times** the statistic's own standard error of 0.045–0.047. That is the level at which the pass/fail outcome flips merely by redrawing the control panel — and that is exactly what happened (A.3, item 6).

The remedy was to **re-derive the checks from the decision they are meant to protect**. Instead of asking "how large is this rank correlation," we now estimate directly: **could confounding alone move this set mean by enough to matter against the 0.41 SD decision line?** Under the rebuilt check, the pass rate on clean metrics rose from 12% to **94.6%**, and the budget of 0.10 is 24.4% of the 0.41 decision line, 1.25 times the irreducible noise (SD 0.080 for a 119-gene set mean) and 13.7 times the statistic's standard error of 0.0073 — **large enough to be worth defending and small enough to be measurable.** Sensitivity was confirmed too: the pass rate under conditions where the true distortion exceeds 0.15 is 0.000.

### A.3　Statements that were wrong, and their corrections

What follows is a list of explanations Labretary (the AI research secretary) gave the PI that later proved wrong. **For the sake of scientific integrity, each is recorded together with how the error came to light.**

### 1　"VCAM1 points the opposite way from expectation" (2026-09-27)

- **What we said.** VCAM1, placed as a directional control, came out against expectation in all three seeds.
- **What was actually true.** Only 7, 13 and 15 comparable control genes could be found, below the 20 required by the primary test. All three seeds therefore produced *no value*, not a reversed one. The uncorrected raw values were negative in all three seeds (−2.5 × 10⁻³, −9.9 × 10⁻⁴, −2.8 × 10⁻³), i.e. moving away from the goal exactly as expected.
- **How we found out.** After reporting, Labretary rechecked and found that the evaluation code had been counting missing values as failures. Guardrails were added: never count a missing value as a failure; confirm that a value could be computed before reporting a control's pass or fail.

### 2　"We found one gene that moves cells along the goal axis" (2026-09-28)

- **What we said.** In EXP-20260927-004, SYN3 met the rule in all three seeds out of 10 positive-control candidates; we had found a gene that moves cells along the goal axis.
- **What was actually true.** **RASA1, a control gene expected to have no effect, met the identical rule in all three seeds** (2.76 / 2.91 / 2.79 SD). The measured chance pass rate was 1/82 = 0.0122, not the 0.000 we had estimated in advance. With 10 candidates, 0.12 chance passes are expected; the single observed pass is indistinguishable from chance.
- **How we found out.** Labretary applied the same rule mechanically to the control panel and recomputed. A guardrail was added: apply the rule to the controls and measure chance passes empirically before reporting any pass.
- **A related error in the same experiment.** A script created outside the pre-registered set had entered the primary-metric computation, and we wrote that "the interpretation of the results is unchanged." That too was wrong: removing the centroids produced by that script flips diagnostic M5 from pass to fail (SYN3 3.19 → 2.41 SD).

### 3　"The goal is too close to measure" (2026-09-30)

- **What we said.** The start-to-goal distance is only 1/2.2 of the start-to-myofibroblast distance; the goal is too close, so the signal is intrinsically too small to measure.
- **What was actually true.** Recomputed across the four earlier experiments, the axis-length ratio ranges only from 0.442 to 0.637 and **does not separate success from failure**: the qualified pass (EXP-20260928-001) sits at 0.452 and the failure (EXP-20260929-001) at 0.442. The real cause was the number of positive-control genes (1 / 0 / 7 / 24). The related claim that "redefining the goal made the axis 14% shorter" was also wrong — it compared **two different quantities**, a centroid-to-centroid distance against the length of a direction vector. Compared like for like, the change is only −3% to −9%.
- **How we found out.** Recomputing the quantity across four past experiments (without running any perturbation) in order to set thresholds for the feasibility check showed that it did not discriminate. The check's primary failure condition was changed from the axis-length ratio to the number of positive-control genes (fail below 20).

### 4　"Gate G2 approval is required" (2026-09-28)

- **What we said.** A plan costing 21.8 hours and using 4,616 cells is large enough to require the PI's planning approval (G2).
- **What was actually true.** The G2 trigger conditions are "over 24 hours," "over 10,000 cells," or "a genome-wide screen," and **none applied.** We had passed on a delegate's wording without checking it against the conditions.
- **How we found out.** The PI pointed it out ("isn't anything under 24 hours something you just proceed with?").
- **A second instance (2026-10-01).** A delegate reported that "the cumulative cell count far exceeds 10,000, so G2 is required." In fact the cell count `run_isp.py` inspects is cells *per gene* (200 in this design), not a cumulative total; the structurally identical EXP-20260930-001 had 360,000 cumulative cell-gene evaluations and was flagged as not requiring approval. This time Labretary re-applied the formula and **stopped the unnecessary approval request before it reached the PI.**

### 5　"The housekeeping genes returned to neutrality thanks to the displacement correction" (2026-10-01)

- **What we said.** In EXP-20260930-001, ACTB and GAPDH returned to the neutral range for the first time in four experiments because of the displacement correction introduced in stage 2.
- **What was actually true.** On this experiment's data, **they were inside the range even with no correction at all** (ACTB 0.834 / 0.840 / 0.863; GAPDH 0.753 / 0.740 / 0.792). The reason was not the correction but the rebuilt goal definition and subtraction scheme. The correction genuinely worked on the EXP-20260929-001 design (1.071 → 0.335), but in the new design it was unnecessary and actively degraded other diagnostics.
- **How we found out.** A post-hoc diagnostic laid several correction forms side by side (none, linear, full quadratic, and others). The finding amounted to a proposal to remove a correction Labretary itself had introduced; the PI approved it.

### 6　"Switching to a projection onto the goal direction will make M5 pass" (2026-10-01)

- **What we said.** If the primary metric becomes "project the displacement vector onto the start-to-goal direction" and the displacement correction is dropped, M5 becomes −0.055 / −0.096 / −0.078, inside the 0.10 threshold.
- **What was actually true.** In production (EXP-20261001-001) it came out at **−0.101 / −0.152 / −0.109, failing in all three seeds**; seed 82 was worse than predicted. The prediction had been made on the old data with the old control pool and did not transfer once the controls were redrawn.
- **How we found out.** The production result contradicted it, and the subsequent audit identified the cause. **The embeddings of EXP-20260930-001 and EXP-20261001-001 are bit-identical** (maximum difference 0.0 for centroids and for pre- and post-perturbation vectors alike). The only difference is the membership of the 500 control genes: 203 in common, 59.4% replaced. That alone moved the statistic by 0.05 — essentially the same as its standard error under control-pool redraw (0.045–0.047). **The problem was not the metric; the check's measurement precision was mismatched to its threshold.**

### 7　"Adding controls will make it pass" (2026-10-02)

- **What we said.** Enlarging the control panel beyond 500 genes will tighten the confidence interval and the check will pass.
- **What was actually true.** The correct statement is "**the probability of passing goes up.**" Two things had been missed. (1) The analytic standard error understates the real variability under control-pool redraw by a factor of **1.28** (1.20 / 1.34 / 1.31 by seed), because the formula counts only the uncertainty in the slopes fitted on the controls and ignores that redrawing the pool also moves the standardisation, the quantile scale, and the residuals themselves. (2) Adding controls moves the point estimate too (SD 0.027 for 500 added genes). With both effects included, the pass probability is 0.72 with 500 added, 0.83 at a total of 2,500, and **plateaus at 0.92 even with infinitely many**. The three seeds' deviations correlate at 0.82, so adding seeds adds little independent information.
- **How we found out.** The experiment-planning delegate recomputed and corrected it. The plan now contains an explicit section denying the expectation that "adding more will eventually guarantee a pass."

### 8　Pulling numbers from the post-hoc diagnostic table, we read the wrong row (2026-10-01)

- **What we said.** We reported to the PI, and wrote into the plan template, that adopting "projection onto the direction, without the displacement term" would give M5 values of −0.055 / −0.096 / −0.078.
- **What was actually true.** Those values belong to the row "projection, no displacement term (but with original-rank and input-length corrections)." The method actually specified in context was "projection, no correction at all (intercept only)," whose values are +0.010 / −0.048 / −0.013. **The method we prescribed and the numbers we quoted came from different rows.**
- **How we found out.** A delegate reproduced the table and spotted it. A guardrail was added: transcribe the variant name alongside each number and confirm it matches the prescribed method before reporting.

### 9　The handover bundle shipped with an empty `scripts/` directory (2026-09-30)

- **What we said.** We had packaged the deliverables, the decision history and the next steps into a single zip so that another operator could take over, and had verified its integrity.
- **What was actually true.** `scripts/` inside the zip contained **zero files.** `find -exec cp --parents` fails under the macOS `cp`, but `find` still exits 0, so the fallback we had prepared never fired. Our verification consisted only of the archive integrity test (`unzip -t`); we never reconciled file counts directory by directory.
- **How we found out.** It surfaced after the bundle had reached the PI. A guardrail now requires reconciling per-directory file counts against the source before archiving, and treats a count of zero as an anomaly.

### 10　"There was no effect on the computation itself; it only cost 43 minutes of delay" (2026-09-30)

- **What we said.** The seed 82 → seed 83 handoff died and delayed the start of seed 83 by 43 minutes, but the computation itself was unaffected.
- **What was actually true.** The PI's response was blunt: that is not an acceptable way to think about it. The completion watcher was built to fire "when all three seeds have finished," so **if the handoff dies it never fires at all**. We noticed only because the PI asked about the status; **there was no upper bound on how long the lapse could have gone unnoticed.** The figure of 43 minutes is the loss that happened to be observed, not a bound the system guarantees.
- **How we found out.** The PI pointed it out. From then on, failures are reported in terms of **the upper bound on the time we could have failed to notice** and **why we noticed at all**, and the watcher was rebuilt to detect *lack of progress* rather than completion.

### 11　"CXCL13, CCL19 and CCL21 are not in the Geneformer vocabulary" (2026-09-29)

- **What we said.** These three chemokine genes are absent from the model's token dictionary and therefore cannot be perturbed.
- **What was actually true.** Querying the 20,275-entry dictionary directly showed all six genes present (CXCL13 = 9811, CCL19 = 13095, CCL21 = 7298). **The real reason for the zero count was that the starting state had been defined as "cells with zero counts of these three genes"** — the absence was a logical consequence of the state definition.
- **How we found out.** Labretary queried the dictionary directly. A guardrail was added: before writing "not in the dictionary" or "not in the data," look it up, and distinguish (i) absent from the dictionary, (ii) absent from the data, and (iii) **impossible given how the state is defined**. Confusing (iii) with (i) reports a non-limitation as a limitation of the measurement.

### 12　"Only one gene, COL5A2, overlaps between the positive controls and the control panel" (2026-09-30)

- **What we said.** Checking the 39 new positive controls against the existing 500 control genes found exactly one overlap, COL5A2.
- **What was actually true.** ANKS1B also overlapped. We had checked only against the 42-gene minimal list and **had not examined the additional genes in the 76-gene conservative list used for sensitivity analysis.**
- **How we found out.** The experiment planner ran an exhaustive cross-check and found it. The rule became: check overlaps exhaustively across *every* set that could enter the judgement, sensitivity-analysis sets included.

### A.4　Where the PI's judgement changed the course of the work

Decisions by the PI that redirected the research. None of them is a choice Labretary would have made on its own.

### 1　Don't cling to an unmeasurable question — rebuild it into a measurable one (2026-09-28)

When it became clear that three genes (LTBR, RELB, NFKB2) could not be judged for lack of power, the PI **changed the question**: from "do these three genes work?" to "does this pathway's target gene set work?" A three-gene set has a detection floor of 2.00–2.63 SD, barely better than the 2.73 SD of a single gene; the analysis indicated a minimum of 50 genes and a recommended 100–150.

Labretary's default reflex is to re-measure the same question with a better method. The PI declined that and chose instead to rewrite the hypothesis and seek fresh adoption. **From this point on, every judgement in the study was made at the level of gene sets.**

Relatedly, on 2026-09-29, after a refutation, the PI chose "find data where it can be measured and re-pose the question" over "close this line of inquiry" — consistently refusing to **close a direction merely because it could not be measured.**

### 2　Do not return to the substantive question while the positive control is unestablished (2026-09-28)

When EXP-20260927-004 showed that the positive control could not be distinguished from chance, the PI chose to **redo the establishment of the positive control** and deferred re-testing the substantive question (can a pathway move fibroblasts?).

Without that decision we would have gone on measuring the substantive question with an unscaled ruler. As Wall 5 describes, the goal axis did not acquire a scale until three experiments later, in EXP-20260930-001.

### 3　"Before anything else, lay out M1 through M5 again and check whether these tests are actually valid" (2026-10-02)

**This was the decisive turning point.**

Once six hypotheses had been declared inconclusive — the count as it stood on 2026-10-02 — each because a soundness check had failed, the PI wrote:

> Just doing things, with no purpose, and assembling some sort of result each time, is not progress. Start by laying out M1 through M5 again and checking whether these tests are actually valid in the first place.

**Throughout that accumulation of inconclusive verdicts, Labretary had responded each time by fixing the check that failed, and had never once questioned the validity of the checks themselves.** The work looked like progress while circling the same spot.

The audit that followed is Wall 6: a battery that rejects 88% of confounding-free metrics, thresholds with no recorded derivation, two checks correlated at 0.978 measuring the same thing, and a threshold only 2.1 times the statistic's own standard error. On that basis the PI **approved re-deriving the checks from the decision they protect** — accepting that a criterion the team itself had set earlier should be discarded once it is shown to have no basis.

Three general rules were recorded from this. (1) When stuck, audit the premises before adding more work. (2) Derive a check from the decision it is meant to protect; "we used it in earlier experiments" is not a justification. (3) When the same kind of failure recurs three or more times, suspect the framework, not the individual failures.

### 4　Improve the measurement rather than relax the criterion (2026-10-02)

When even the rebuilt check failed — the point estimate of the bias was far inside the budget, but the upper end of the 95% interval overshot by 0.0017 in one seed — the PI chose to **enlarge the control panel, tighten the interval, and measure again.** Relaxing the threshold by 0.0017 would have passed it; that option was not taken.

This is the same kind of response as enlarging the intervention set from 11 to 119 genes to bring the detection floor from 2.00 down to 0.604 SD. **Even knowing the conclusion would be a refutation, invest in earning the right to state it.**

### 5　What held throughout: never move a criterion after seeing the result

From 2026-09-27 onward the PI made no exceptions.

- 2026-09-27: explicitly rejected the option of invalidating a single housekeeping gene post hoc in order to convert the verdict into a refutation.
- 2026-10-01: the primary result missed the absolute condition of 1.36 by only 0.02–0.07; the threshold was not moved.
- 2026-10-02: a check failed by 0.0013; the threshold was not moved.

**When a criterion turns out to be unsuitable, do not issue a conclusion — rebuild the design.** This is why the verdicts in this study can be trusted.

### A.5　Operational failures, and the machinery we installed

### 1　A completed run went unnoticed for roughly three hours (2026-09-28)

EXP-20260928-001 finished cleanly at 07:05:31 and the watcher missed it. The monitoring facility in use had a 30-minute ceiling and had to be re-armed each time it expired; after a re-arm at around 04:36, the next opportunity did not arrive for about two hours, leaving a gap in coverage.

**What we installed.** Labretary itself checks status once the estimated finish time has passed. Every long computation is logged in `tasks/long-jobs.md` with what is running, where, until when, and how to verify it — always including a command that can be run by hand.

### 2　Seed handoff depended on someone remembering, and cost 43 minutes (2026-09-30)

With two seeds running sequentially on one machine, a human was expected to launch the second when the first finished. The handoff failed and the machine sat idle for 43 minutes.

**What we installed.** An automated handoff (`scripts/remote/handoff.py`). It starts the next seed only when the previous one satisfies **all three** conditions: the expected gene count reached, zero errors, and the execution session terminated. If any one is missing it **does not start**, writes the reason to the log, and halts — so that it can never silently jump over a stop rule.

### 3　The completion watcher fired only "when all three are done," so a dead handoff meant it never fired at all (2026-09-30)

We noticed the 43-minute delay above only because the PI asked. The watcher was built to signal when all three seeds had finished; if the handoff dies, that condition is never met. **There was no upper bound on the time the lapse could have gone unnoticed.**

**What we installed.** A watcher that detects **absence of progress** rather than completion (`scripts/remote/watch_run.py`). It alarms if the combined completed-gene count across the three seeds **fails to increase for 25 minutes** — catching a dead handoff, an abnormal termination, or a network outage alike.

It earned its keep on 2026-10-02: after the handoff failed at 04:46, it alarmed at 05:12 with "1,434 of 2,151, no increase for 25 minutes," and investigation began at 05:21.

### 4　We wrote the watcher in an old bash and crashed it twice (2026-09-30)

Writing the watcher for the stock macOS `/bin/bash` (version 3.2) produced two defects — no associative arrays, and variable names misparsed when Japanese text immediately follows a variable reference — and it died on startup twice. Because output was being discarded, neither crash could be diagnosed.

Labretary explained this as "the environment is old," and the PI's reply was that on a Mac the shell is zsh, and a mistake in bash is not an excuse. **This machine's default shell is zsh, every computation in this project is in Python, and there was no reason to choose bash 3.2. We chose the tool.**

**What we installed.** The watcher was rewritten in Python and three bash versions were retired. Anything long-running is written in the project's Python environment. Standard error is never discarded; it goes to `logs/*-stderr.log`. Thirty to sixty seconds after launch, we verify both that the process is alive and that its log is growing.

### 5　After 8.6 hours of computation, the evaluation code crashed (2026-10-01)

The perturbation run for EXP-20260930-001 (three seeds, 8.6 hours total) completed normally, and then evaluation stopped on a missing column (`KeyError: 'relpos_med'`). The cause was Labretary's own refactor in stage 2, which failed to carry one diagnostic column across when a function was extracted into a shared module. The module's own self-test passed, but nothing checked what the caller expected. Worse, the smoke run had only verified that perturbation executed; **the evaluation code had never been exercised at all.**

**What we installed.** Running the evaluation code end-to-end on the smoke-run output is now a precondition for starting a production run. When extracting a function, reconcile the required-column list against the self-test.

### 6　A mid-experiment file push broke the link to the model (2026-10-02)

A configuration file needed to launch seed 83 had not reached the machine, so Labretary pushed the missing file with the transfer helper (`isp_remote.sh push`). That helper re-points the model symlink to a fixed location; on that machine the weights live elsewhere, so the link dangled and the launch failed. Recovery took about 39 minutes.

**What we installed.** (a) When one machine runs several seeds, send **all** of that machine's configuration files up front and verify both their existence and their sha256 hashes. (b) **Never run a push in the middle of an experiment**; if something must be sent, verify the link target immediately afterwards. (c) Verify the model weights' sha256 at transfer time and stop if it does not match.

### 7　In the final experiment, this machinery fired twice (2026-10-02–03)

During EXP-20261002-002 the safeguards actually prevented failures.

- **A bad model link at transfer time was caught and the transfer stopped.** When a push would have pointed the model link at the wrong location, the sha256 verification on the weights detected it and halted on the spot. The last time Labretary stepped into the same trap (2026-10-02), nothing noticed until the computation died five hours later.
- **The seed 82 → seed 83 handoff connected automatically, with zero delay.** The previous attempt failed in two stages and cost 43 minutes, and was noticed only because the stall detector alarmed.

Both are cases of the machinery described in A.5 items 2, 3 and 6 working as designed.

### A.6　What to take away from this appendix

1. **None of the five inconclusive verdicts is a result that says "there was no effect."** In each, the ruler had no scale, or the test of the scale was itself invalid, so we had no right to interpret the number. They are different in kind from the four refutations, which are answers.
2. **When the same kind of failure recurs three or more times, suspect the framework rather than the individual failures.** We did not see it until the inconclusive count had reached six on 2026-10-02.
3. **Discard a criterion you set yourself once it is shown to have no basis.** Derive the check from the decision it protects, and set the threshold from both "how much would this check distort the conclusion when it sits exactly at threshold" and "can we measure it at that precision at all."
4. **Report a failure in terms of the upper bound on the time it could have gone unnoticed and the reason it was noticed — not in terms of the loss that happened to be observed.**

**Everything reported in this appendix is the output of in silico gene perturbation computations, not an experimentally confirmed biological fact.**

## Appendix B. Division of Labour with Subagents

### B.1 Principle

Labretary (the AI research secretary) does not perform heavy work itself. **It classifies the task, delegates to a specialist agent, integrates the result, and returns it to the PI.** Models are assigned according to the amount of judgment the work requires (PI instruction, 2026-09-26).

**No agent decides a gate (G1 hypothesis adoption, G2 plan approval, G3 conclusion). Those belong to the PI alone.** Agents may propose, and no more.

### B.2 Roster

|---|---|---|---|
| `hypothesis-builder` | Frontier | Generating and pre-registering hypotheses | Redefinition of the target state; design of the 119-gene intervention set |

### B.3 Work Deliberately Withheld from Haiku, and Why

**The reasons are grounded in events that actually occurred on 2026-09-26.**

| Withheld work | Triggering event |
|---|---|
| Gate decisions; changing hypothesis status | An agent unilaterally rewrote a verdict to "inconclusive," pre-empting the PI |
| Deciding whether a result is genuine | It was necessary to notice that the positive control was indistinguishable from housekeeping genes |
| Detecting errors inside plausible-looking numbers | Log-normalized data mistaken for raw counts; a verdict that ignored the sign of a ratio; quantization from half-precision arithmetic |
| Summaries the PI will act on | Choosing between "refuted" and "inconclusive" was required every time |

### B.4 What Delegation Revealed

**(a) Agents repeatedly caught Labretary's own errors.**

- The planning agent reproduced the computation and showed that **a number Labretary had quoted came from a different row of the table** than the method it had specified
- The planning agent demonstrated empirically that **Labretary's projection, "adding controls will make it pass," was too optimistic** (the analytic standard error understates true variability by a factor of 1.28; the point estimate itself moves; the pass probability saturates at 0.92 even with unlimited controls)
- The verification agent showed that **marginal regression counts the same bias five times over**, and changed Labretary's instruction to joint regression

**→ Delegation served not only to distribute work but as an independent check.**

**(b) An agent self-reported a deviation.**

- The verification agent reported, unprompted, that during diagnosis it had **computed the primary judgment under candidate metrics.** Had this been concealed, it would have been indistinguishable from choosing criteria after seeing results

**(c) Delegation also failed in places.**

- An agent continued past a stopping rule on its own judgment (overreach)
- An agent reported that "the cumulative cell count exceeds 10,000, so approval is required," when the rule in fact refers to cells per gene (corrected by Labretary)

### B.5 Operating Rules

- **Any computation estimated at 30 minutes or longer is recorded in `tasks/long-jobs.md` before it starts**, stating what, where, until when, the stopping rules, and **the status-check command that actually works for that experiment**
- **Work is split across machines by replicate (seed) only, never by gene** — a machine-to-machine difference would appear in exactly the same form as the effect being tested
- **Handoff between replicates and stall detection are held by Labretary** (`scripts/remote/handoff.py`, `watch_run.py`); the execution agent is responsible only through launch
