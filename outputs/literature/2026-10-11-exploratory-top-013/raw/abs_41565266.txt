TITLE: From Carbonic Anhydrase 12 to TGFβ1/Smad Pathway: A Key Mechanism for Isoorientin in Pulmonary Fibrosis Therapy.
ABSTRACT: An active component of traditional Chinese medicine, isoorientin (IRT), has been shown to relieve liver fibrosis, which triggers the investigation regarding its impact on pulmonary fibrosis (PF) and the associated mechanism. An in vitro PF model was induced in rat type II alveolar RLE-6TN cells by treatment with transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1), before which IRT (10 µM, 20 µM and 40 µM) pretreatment was performed. The effects of IRT on cell survival and apoptosis were detected. Western blot was used to analyze the expressions of fibrotic markers and TGF-β1/Smad2 pathway proteins in cells. An RLE-6TN cell line with carbonic anhydride enzyme 12 (CA12) knockdown was created to further explore the mechanism by which IRT improves PF. IRT (10, 20, and 40 µM) pretreatment did not induce cytotoxicity in RLE-6TN cells, but reversed the effects of TGF-β1 exposure on RLE-6TN cells, including apoptosis promotion, viability decreases, downregulation of CA12, upregulation of TGF-β1, α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) and Collagen I, and activation of the TGF-β1/Smad2 pathway. CA12 silencing enhanced the effect of TGF-β1 and attenuated the reversing effects of IRT (40 µM) pretreatment on TGF-β1. IRT (40 µM) pretreatment, in turn, offset the promoting role of CA12 silencing in TGF-β1. CA12 overexpression alleviated TGF-β1-induced damage, consistent with the therapeutic effects of IRT. In conclusion, IRT alleviates PF through CA12-induced suppression of the TGFβ1/Smad signaling pathway.
